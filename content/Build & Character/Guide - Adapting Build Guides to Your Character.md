## Reading Build Planners

Build planners are static snapshots of a final product — they don't show the progression path. Creators adapt on the fly while leveling/gathering gear; the guide won't capture that.

Cliptis structures his guides in phases: **Starter (legendary)** → **Unique** → **Mythic** → **Final (tankiest, highest gear reqs)**. Each phase has different gear requirements and trade-offs. Know which phase you're in and whether you can actually equip what the planner shows.

## Paragon Board Progression

The most common mistake: maxing out glyphs and filling every node on early boards before connecting all boards.

At paragon 200, you can reach every glyph and every legendary node — but only if you take **minimal paths**. If you fill out everything, you run out of points before connecting all boards and miss damage/survivability nodes.

Cliptis shows intermediate paragon levels (229 → 276 → full board) to demonstrate progression. Use intermediate board snapshots when available. Once you understand how boards are built, you can adapt even when the guide doesn't provide every step.

## Adapting Gear: Offense vs. Defense

Build guides can't know your character's current state. Two rules:

- **Dying constantly?** Swap offensive affixes for defensive ones. Maximum life instead of strength. Damage reduction instead of crit damage. You can't do damage if you're dead.
- **Never dying but can't kill?** Swap defensive affixes for offensive ones. Strip some life/armor for more multipliers.

This is explicitly okay. The guide is the destination — adapt the path.

## Keyword Search in Skill Tree + Paragon

Both the skill tree and paragon board now have a **keyword search** at the top. Use it:

- **Skill tree**: Type "damage reduction", "life", "defenses" — it highlights every matching node. Grab the ones you missed.
- **Paragon board**: Same thing. Type "life", "armor", "resistances" to find nodes you need for survival. Glyphs like **Ire** (damage reduction from elites) can be temporarily swapped in while gearing up, then replaced once you have better gear.

## Codex of Power for Defensive Gaps

Open the Occultist → Codex of Power → search "damage reduction". Shows every aspect with DR on it. Swap offensive aspects for defensive ones while you bridge gear gaps. Craft replacements in the Horadric Cube. Remove defensive aspects once your gear catches up.

## Additive vs. Multiplicative Damage

Two damage types, one relationship:

- **Additive (+)** — All additive bonuses lump into one bucket. Having 25,000–35,000% additive damage looks impressive but means little if it's only run through one or two multipliers.
- **Multiplicative [x]** — Each multiplier is its own separate channel. Your additive bucket runs through EVERY multiplier.

The math: additive gets run through multiplier × multiplier × multiplier. More multipliers = exponential scaling. But you still need additive — multipliers with nothing to multiply produce nothing.

Blizzard made this easier in Lord of Hatred by stripping most additive damage off gear and clarifying tooltips (now explicitly says "physical damage multipliers" with [x]).

### Practical Demonstration (Cliptis, Whirlwind Barb)

All numbers Torment 12, same buffs, same dummies:

| Setup | Damage |
|---|---|
| Full build (all multipliers, correct affixes) | ~27 trillion |
| Ramaladni's aspect reduced by 12% (same affixes, same diamonds) | ~11–13 trillion |
| Gohr's grips swapped off (lost one full multiplier, ~15% loss) | **Sub-1 trillion** (no T's at all) |

12% on one affix = damage halved. One multiplier removed = damage collapsed. This is why affix precision and multiplier count matter more than item power.

## Masterworking Priority

Target the **single most important affix** for the build with masterworking crits. Cliptis's example: chest piece with +40 maximum resources. Max resources → Melted Heart of Selig (doubled) → Ramaladni's (damage scales with max resources). Missing masterworking crits on that one affix cascades through the entire damage chain.

Each piece of gear should have one greater affix — the build-defining one. Tempers are separate (keep re-rolling for greater affix tempers).

### Masterworking Adaptation

Same rule as gear: if you're dying, put masterworking into max life or armor instead of the prescribed offensive stat. You can re-masterwork later once gear improves.

## Glyph Levels Matter

Going from glyph level 51 to 100 is a significant damage increase. Level 51 to 150 is massive. If the guide creator has level 150 glyphs and yours are 51, that alone explains a large damage gap.
